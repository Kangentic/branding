Priority meter contract

kangentic #1160 signed off the look on 2026-11-22: equal bars, one per level above None, lit up to the task's level, 3px bars with 2px gaps, 10px tall, the level's color, unlit at 30% and None at 45%. This card makes the package own those numbers, so the desktop, the web demo and the phone draw one meter.

The look is settled and is not reopened here. Rendering it on every ground the canvas did not show turned up one new finding: at 125%, 150% and 175% display scaling the four bars are not equal (section 3). The rest of this page is the contract and where the meter sits on each surface.

Shipped. assets/priority/meter-0.svg, assets/priority/meter-1.svg, assets/priority/meter-2.svg, assets/priority/meter-3.svg, assets/priority/meter-4.svg, assets/priority/meter.json, gated by the PRIORITY invariant check and recorded in .claude/rules/priority-meter-geometry.md.

Signed off 2026-11-22: 1B, 2A, 3 as drawn, 4A, 5B

The record, from DECISIONS in scripts/lib/priority.mjs: each question, the pick, and every rejected direction with its reason.

  1. How the bars land on device pixels. Picked B: Whole device pixels: pixelSnap(), round half up. Rejected A, Layout pixels, as #1160 drew them: Unequal bars at every fractional scale, measured in Chromium (4 4 4 3 at 125%, 4 5 4 5 at 150%, 6 5 5 5 at 175%), which defeats 3B's equal bars on the usual Windows laptop settings.
  2. Where the phone puts the meter. Picked A: The title row, after #N and before the PR glyph: the desktop's order (1A). Rejected B, Leading the labels row, where the flag pill was: A card with no labels would still need the row, which #1146 removed on the desktop, and it breaks parity with the desktop's title row.
  3. The contract's shape. Picked as drawn: meter.json (numbers, rules, pixelSnap, maxLevels, worked examples) plus meter-0.svg to meter-4.svg for the default scale.
  4. Who picks the color. Picked A: The level's own color, as the user picks it, through currentColor. Rejected B, One fixed tone for every level: An Urgent card stops standing out from a Low one except by two bars, and it is a kangentic change (the picker goes, PriorityBadge stops reading entry.color) plus a phone change if the protocol drops the colors. Rejected ramp, A fixed per-level color ramp owned by this package (not drawn): This package ships no color, only currentColor geometry, because the desktop, the phone and the website each tune color to their own grounds (design-language).
  5. How long a scale can get. Picked B: A cap of 7 levels (MAX_LEVELS), stated in meter.json for kangentic's Priorities popover to enforce (a follow-up there). Rejected A, No cap: The meter grows 5px per level; a 15-level scale draws a 68px meter, which crowds the title of a 224px card.

3. Pixel truth, and the one new finding

High (3 of 4 lit) on the dark card, rasterized at each display scale and enlarged 6x with no smoothing. Each cell lists the four bars' widths in device pixels. A span's background and a View's frame have every edge rounded to a device pixel, so row A is what plain 3px bars draw: equal at 1x and 2x, unequal at 125%, 150% and 175%, the usual Windows laptop settings, with the meter starting on a whole pixel as this sheet places it. Android's 2.75 and 3.5 do the same. Row B is what ships: pixelSnap() sizes every bar in whole device pixels.

A. Layout pixels, as #1160 drew themrejected

Bars are 3, gaps 2, height 10 in CSS px or dp, and the renderer rounds every edge. Equal at 1x, 2x and 3x; one device px apart at 1.25x, 1.5x, 1.75x, 2.75x and 3.5x.

B. Whole device pixelsshipped

Bar, gap and height are each rounded ONCE to whole device px, half up, then every bar uses them. Equal at every scale. The meter's layout width moves by up to 2px with the scale (20 at 1.25x, 19.33 at 1.5x, against 18).

Measured in Chromium, 2026-11-22, not only modeled: a real title row (title, #N, then the span meter), with CSS zoom standing in for the display scale. Row A painted 4 4 4 3 at 125%, 4 5 4 5 at 150%, 6 5 5 5 at 175%. Row B painted 4 4 4 4 at 125%, 5 5 5 5 at 150%, 5 5 5 5 at 175%. Which bar comes out wide depends on where the meter starts on the row, so the sheet above shows one placement and a card shows another; under A some bar is always off. The phone's 2.75x and 3.5x are modeled from Yoga's edge rounding, not measured here.

Why B. It keeps the signed-off look exactly at 1x, 2x and 3x, and makes it true at every other scale. Each of bar, gap and height is rounded once, half up, because 1.5x bar width (4.5) and 1.25x gap (2.5) are exact ties. The phone gets it for one call (PixelRatio.roundToNearestPixel(3)). The desktop needs devicePixelRatio and a listener for a monitor change, a small addition to #1160's PriorityMeter.tsx. The reference SVG (last row) does not snap at all: it is soft at every fractional scale, which is why it is a reference and not what a card draws.

4. In place

Each surface as its consumer builds it: system face, 14px title on a 20px row, the meter centered on that row. Measured 2026-11-22 in Chromium on Windows: the 14px title's baseline sits at 15 and its capital height is 10, so the cap band is 5 to 15, exactly where the 10px meter sits. From their metrics, SF Pro's cap band centers 0.04px low and Roboto's 0.19px high.

5. Level 0, and what a consumer supplies

A card draws nothing at level 0 and keeps no slot (2B); the phone's priorityLevelFor already returns null there. A list or picker that names every level, such as the Backlog pill, the Priorities popover and an automation's Priority condition, draws level 0 as every bar at 0.45. The meter is decorative everywhere: the element around it carries the name ("Priority: High"), as the desktop's badge and the phone's pill already do. Past the end of the scale, the consumer supplies its fallback gray, as both already do for P<n>.

A. No caprejected

The rule stays as signed off: one bar per level, however many. A project that wants fifteen levels gets a 68px meter and short titles. Nothing changes in kangentic.

B. Cap the scale at 7 levelsshipped

7 levels draw 6 bars, 28px. meter.json states the cap as maxLevels: 7, for kangentic's Priorities popover to disable Add Priority there. That is a kangentic change, a follow-up there, and a project that already has more keeps them: the meter still draws by the rule, it just cannot grow further. One bar per level stays true.

6. Who picks the color (decision 4)

The meter has no color of its own. Every reference SVG is currentColor and meter.json carries no hex, so a level draws in whatever color it holds: a swatch from the Priorities popover, or any hex from its custom picker. It is extensible the same way. The bar count comes from the rule, not a fixed glyph, so a sixth level gives five bars (section 1, any scale length).

A. The level's color, as the user picks itshipped

Today's model, and what #1160 signed off. No kangentic change beyond #1160 importing the numbers: PriorityMeter already takes color={entry.color}, and the phone already receives each level's color over the protocol. Urgent reads red from across the board. The cost of a free choice: a color close to the ground thins the gap between lit and unlit bars, as the custom slate shows on dark and the pale yellow on light. Labels carry the same risk today.

B. One fixed tone for every levelrejected

Every level draws in the row's muted text tone, and the lit count alone carries the rank. A kangentic change: the popover's color picker goes, PriorityBadge stops reading entry.color, and each level's stored color becomes dead data unless the protocol drops it, which is a phone change too. The meter is legible on every theme by construction, but an Urgent card no longer stands out from a Low one except by two bars.

7. The contract (decision 3)

  • assets/priority/meter.json: the numbers, the rules as expressions, pixelSnap with a worked table of 9 display scales, maxLevels: 7, placement, level 0 and accessibility, and a worked table of 18 cases across scales of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 levels that a consumer's tests can assert against.
  • assets/priority/meter-0.svg: None, 0 of 4 lit
  • assets/priority/meter-1.svg: Low, 1 of 4 lit
  • assets/priority/meter-2.svg: Medium, 2 of 4 lit
  • assets/priority/meter-3.svg: High, 3 of 4 lit
  • assets/priority/meter-4.svg: Urgent, 4 of 4 lit

Each SVG is meter.json's bars drawn literally: one <rect> per bar at integer coordinates, currentColor, with fill-opacity on the unlit bars. They are a reference: the desktop draws spans and the phone draws Views from meter.json, because the phone's activity renderer would stroke these rects as outlines and drop their opacity. The PRIORITY invariant check holds the shipped files to the lib and recomputes both worked tables from the json's own numbers; .claude/rules/priority-meter-geometry.md is the binding record.

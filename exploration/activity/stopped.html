activity marks / Kangentic #994

Agent stopped mark

A fourth amber mark for a card the board has stopped: the agent finished, and nothing will move this card until a person acts. Nothing went wrong, so it must not read as a failure. It also must not read as the stop button.

The flag is selected. It joins the set as agent-stopped, the twelfth mark. It is an outline with no ring, no fill and no centred glyph, which keeps it away from the stop button.

The shield is quartered. The envelope and the bubble read apart because of the line inside the box. The bubble and the shield did not, because both were an empty outline with a small point at the bottom. So agent-approval now has two opposite quarters solid. Its outline has not changed.

The counting surfaces do not change. The desktop sidebar and the Agent Monitor keep one idle count drawn with the envelope, as round 6 decided, so a stopped agent counts there like any other idle one.

The shield, before and after

Solid against empty is the strongest interior a mark can have, and the bubble is empty. The quartering is a heraldic division, drawn on this shield with its outline unchanged.

The shield in true pixels

Before and after, rasterized at true size and enlarged eight times, between the bubble and the two other ambers.

How far the shield moved

This uses the same measure as the flag table further down, with 0 the same drawing and 1 sharing no ink. It can rule a drawing out and it cannot choose one: the band moved the shield further from both the bubble and the envelope, and it still looked like an acorn.

The shield needs no code change

The desktop picks it up with the package, and the phone with its next branding sync. The solid parts are drawn as overlapping strokes in one path, not as a fill. The phone's branding sync refuses a path with its own fill, so a real fill would have stopped its next package update. Compared with a true fill, the strokes leave no seams at any size and differ by about 1% at indicator sizes. The one visible difference is a slightly rounded inner corner where the quarters meet, at 48px and up.

Every line, including the band, every outline-only idea, and half solid. Each keeps its reason in the record.

shield-spine

shield-pale

shield-heater

shield-wedge

shield-badge

How far apart they render

This measures shape alone, with colour and motion removed. 0 is the same drawing and 1 shares no ink. A new mark should sit at least as far from its nearest neighbour as the shipped ambers sit from each other. Figures in the accent colour fall short of that. The shield's one low figure is against the ring, and on a real card tone and motion separate those two. The octagons are low against both the ring and the shield, and the shield is the same amber and just as still, so nothing else would separate them.

Pixel truth

Each mark rasterized at true size and enlarged eight times, so these are the real pixels. They come from librsvg, so a true-size band above is the authority on how Chromium paints a mark.

The stopped marks not chosen

Hand and person reached review with the flag. The other five came out on the true-pixel sheet first. The ring-based and control-shaped ideas were never drawn, because five of a card's glyphs are already circles and the stop button is a ring around a square.

hand

The maintainer chose flag from the card lineup, over this mark, which the page recommended.

person

The maintainer chose flag from the card lineup. The page had stated this one's risk as reading like an assignee.

octagon

At true size the chamfers round off and it renders as a closed circle: distance 0.31 from the ring at 12px and 0.30 at 16px, where the closest shipped amber pair was 0.57 and 0.56 when this was measured, on a card with five other circle-based glyphs. The desktop also draws its CAUTION callout as an octagon, which is the failure reading this mark has to avoid.

octagon-faceted

The deeper chamfer was meant to give 12px visible facets and made it worse: distance 0.18 from the ring at 12px, the closest pair measured in this round.

barrier

It clears the distance bar and reads as a stool: nothing about a rail on two posts says a way is shut without the stripes a 12px render cannot hold.

halted

It breaks the brief's own rule that the mark must not read as a button: an arrow against a line is the jump-to-end and collapse-panel control. Its shaft on y 12 also straddles two pixel rows at 12px, and any other y puts the arrow off centre.

anchor

Its ring, stock and shank merge at 12px, and the interior it needs is what rounds 1 and 2 ruled out.

# Overseer motion

Every animation `@kangentic/branding` ships. Generated by
`scripts/gen-sprites.mjs` from the `SEQUENCES` declaration in
`scripts/lib/sprite.mjs` - the same source the shipped
`assets/mascot/animations.json` and `animations.css` come from, so what you
see here is what consumers get. Do not hand-edit.

Each clip below is a looping GIF at 10x. One-shot sequences hold on the
canonical frame at the end of the loop so they read the way a consumer fires
them. GIFs cannot reproduce the randomised idle gaps, so looping sequences are
rendered at the mean of their range - a consumer reading `animations.json`
gets the real right-skewed cadence.

For the interactive version (real display sizes, click to replay), open
`exploration/mascot/animation-preview.html` in a browser.

## blinking

![blinking](motion/blink-loop.gif)

`blink-loop` &middot; loops

idle life. The human-blink model: gaps clustered short with the odd long pause rather than a flat spread, plus an occasional double blink. The blinks-per-minute figure is always derived from this range wherever it is displayed, never written down, so tuning the range cannot strand a stale number

**Timing:** idle `2000-7000ms` right-skewed (mean 3667ms) &middot; `blink` 140ms &middot; 30% chance of a double

## single arm wave

![single arm wave](motion/wave-once.gif)

`wave-once` &middot; one-shot

a greeting, or the payoff when a connection lands. One arm, five steps. The key stays `wave-once` because kangentic-mobile's animation union already uses that string

**Timing:** `rest` 120ms > `wave` 120ms > `rest` 120ms > `wave` 120ms > `rest` 120ms

## double arm wave

![double arm wave](motion/double-arm-wave-once.gif)

`double-arm-wave-once` &middot; one-shot

both arms up together: he is trying to get your attention. The urgent one - it says 'over here', not 'hello'. Same 120ms step rate as the single arm wave, because it is the same mechanic on the same body part

**Timing:** `rest` 120ms > `arms-up` 120ms > `rest` 120ms > `arms-up` 120ms > `rest` 120ms

## double arm alternating wave

![double arm alternating wave](motion/double-arm-alternating-wave-once.gif)

`double-arm-alternating-wave-once` &middot; one-shot

arms pump alternately rather than together, which reads as playful where the double arm wave reads as urgent. Kept as its own sequence, not an alternate: the two say different things

**Timing:** `rest` 120ms > `wave` 120ms > `wave-left` 120ms > `wave` 120ms > `rest` 120ms

## looking left and right

![looking left and right](motion/looking-left-and-right-loop.gif)

`looking-left-and-right-loop` &middot; loops

the gaze shifts side to side, looking for something that has not arrived yet. The base waiting idle. Deliberately long dwells - a wait screen loops indefinitely, so this has to read as calm, not as a twitch

**Timing:** `rest` 900ms > `look` 900ms

## running

![running](motion/running-loop.gif)

`running-loop` &middot; loops

legs cycling on the spot, which reads as running. Middle foot up, then the outer two. Chosen over an alternating outer-foot march at the 2026-07-25 review - with three legs and a planted centre, a march reads as nothing recognisable

**Timing:** `step-a` 400ms > `step-b` 400ms

## waiting

![waiting](motion/waiting-loop.gif)

`waiting-loop` &middot; loops

the legs never stop - he runs on the spot the whole time, glancing left and right as he goes. Permanent running means the legs are baked into every frame, so at the 4-frame motion budget this carries running plus ONE other behaviour; the gaze won it over the arms. Fire a wave sequence separately if the wait needs a bigger signal

**Timing:** `step-a` 400ms > `step-b` 400ms > `step-a` 400ms > `step-b` 400ms > `step-a-look` 400ms > `step-b-look` 400ms > `step-a-look` 400ms > `step-b-look` 400ms

## Rules

- Sequences are a **stepped swap between 2-4 poses**, never a tween. Pixels hop;
  they do not glide.
- Every sequence rests on the canonical frame under
  `prefers-reduced-motion` - reduced motion is a rendering, not a mute button.
  So **mount every name in the sequence's `mountFrames`**, which is the played
  clip PLUS that resting frame. Mounting only what the clip names renders
  nothing at all once motion is off.
- Display at **integer multiples** of the 18x12 grid only.
  Fractional scaling blurs the pixels and kills the craft.
- Exactly **one frame is visible at a time**. Some poses vacate pixels the rest
  frame paints, so layering a pose over rest lets the old arm or foot bleed
  through; each frame's `compositing` field in the manifest records which.
